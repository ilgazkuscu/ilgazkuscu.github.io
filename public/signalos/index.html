SignalOS · Ilgaz Kuscu

Political signals from news

SignalOS reads each news report, turns it into a change in probability, and compares the result with the Polymarket price for “Will Trump announce the end of U.S. military operations against Iran by April 30?” A YES share pays $1 if it happens by the deadline, so its price is the crowd's probability.

Every April deadline

Brier score: the squared gap between a probability and the outcome, averaged over days. 0 is perfect; 0.25 is always saying 50%.

B = (1/N) Σd (pd − o)²

pd is the probability on day d, o is 1 if YES and 0 if NO, N the days with a price. Example: April 30 resolved NO, so a 30% estimate scores 0.30² = 0.09 that day.

Every news record

Prices are end-of-day values for the April 30 contract. Effect is the change in SignalOS, in percentage points (pts), when the report arrived.

Pipeline

Seven steps, the same for live news and for the replay.

  1. PullTwelve sources are polled every 60 seconds: White House, Department of Defense, IAEA, New York Times, Wall Street Journal, BBC, Financial Times, Foreign Affairs, Brookings, Carnegie, Atlantic Council. Each item is saved with headline, link, publisher and the time SignalOS first saw it.
  2. CleanOne canonical link per article; the same headline at the same link counts once. A record is used only after the time it was seen, so no later news leaks into a day.
  3. FilterRecords about Iran are kept; off-topic, headline-less or link-less records are left out.
  4. ClassifyRules assign a category (diplomatic talks, force posture, proxy attacks, official end wording) and a direction: toward YES or toward NO.
  5. WeighEach record gets an evidence weight w (below).
  6. UpdateWeights move a daily hazard rate λ, which gives the probability for every deadline.
  7. ScoreHourly Polymarket YES prices from the Polymarket price API; both scored with the Brier score.

Evidence weight

The push of one report, as one product of named factors.

w = s · f · c · r · k · (1 − pcontra) · (1 − pcorr),   r = 0.5t/h

s signed size (negative toward NO), f category weight, c confidence, r recency after t hours with half-life h, k profile multiplier, last two: penalties for contradicting reports and repeats of the same story.

High-frequency traders act on scored news feeds of this kind, such as RavenPack news analytics (von Beschwitz, Keim and Massa). The repeat penalty reflects that investors overreact to stale news and the move reverses the next week (Tetlock, Review of Financial Studies 2011).

Hazard rate

The daily chance the announcement happens, given it has not yet; summed over the days to a deadline it gives the probability by that deadline.

P(T) = p0 + (1 − p0)(1 − e−λT),   λ = 0.018 + 0.085 L

T days to the deadline, p0 starting probability, L the sum of evidence weights. Example: with no news, T = 30 gives 1 − e−0.54 = 0.417.

Credit desks price default with the same equation to value credit default swaps (Duffie and Singleton 1999). One λ keeps every deadline consistent: a later deadline is never priced below an earlier one.

Market price

Prices of contracts far from expiry lean toward 50%: unlikely outcomes trade too high, the favorite-longshot bias (Page and Clemen, Economic Journal 2013). Every April deadline here resolved NO after Polymarket priced it well above zero.
